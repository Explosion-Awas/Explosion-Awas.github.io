Abstract

Lens-parameter scans require a new complex transfer function at each dimensionless lens configuration, making preparation and first-band evaluation a distinct computational workload. We construct a direct frequency-domain evaluator for the scalar thin-lens amplification of a standard untruncated Navarro–Frenk–White profile. An explicit modified-Bessel endpoint integral is combined with a complete complex straight-line integral that retains the lens-potential exponential. Starting from the original two-dimensional Gaussian-Abel integral, we prove the finite-contour decomposition, including its retained vertical tail, and then show how extending the line absorbs that tail exactly once. The identities hold for positive frequency and nonzero source momentum. The implementation is tested on 408 frequency samples from 24 lens configurations and six complete 801-frequency bands. All six bands satisfy a 10−1010^{-10}10−10 full-complex error target and a one-percent integrated diffraction-residual target with the stated empirical reference margins. On these bands, the numerical representation meets a joint complex-field and diffraction-residual target beyond the accuracy of the tested image-plus-cusp and secant approximations. The timing comparison uses a common 10−810^{-8}10−8 full-complex and one-percent residual target. Paired comparisons on four of the six tested bands give median preparation-plus-first-band cost ratios of 3.66–6.34 in favor of the direct evaluator. The resulting evaluator targets first-band calculations for changing dimensionless lens models.

1. Introduction

Coherent gravitational lensing requires a complex transfer function, including its frequency-dependent phase. Weak-lensing wave-optics features have been studied as a source of information about lenses, with lens-model dependence and parameter degeneracies affecting the interpretation.[3]

Numerical cost depends on which quantities change between requests. A frequency-domain table for a fixed lens can make later evaluations inexpensive. A scan over lens strength and source offset instead asks for a new transfer function at each new dimensionless pair. This paper studies the preparation and first-band cost of that latter operation. Section 7 specifies which parameter changes require a new dimensionless response.

Complex deformations and nonperturbative resummations are established approaches to lensing diffraction integrals.[1]

Endpoint/saddle organization and efficient constructions of steepest-descent contours also precede this work.[2]

Single-image time-domain descriptions provide another nonperturbative treatment of wave-optics features.[3]

GLoW implements time-domain methods, singular/regular splitting, Fourier transforms and interpolation, including support for NFW lenses.[4][5][7]

We provide a specialized organization for the NFW potential: an explicit K0K_0K0​ endpoint contribution, a straight line on the correct analytic sheet, direct treatment of the weak complex residual, and an empirical refinement/fallback rule. It avoids a finite expansion of the potential exponential and does not require a generic contour-flow search. We derive this representation directly from the original diffraction integral.

The analytical weak-lensing prescription of Wu et al. is a separate relevant comparison. Its central potential is replaced by a frequency-dependent secant construction, combined with a local image correction.[8] We reproduce the v2 formula with exact image geometry, distinguish it from an image2 replacement, and use the same frozen complex reference values for all comparisons. Accuracy of a finite asymptotic approximation and cost of a numerical method are assessed separately.
